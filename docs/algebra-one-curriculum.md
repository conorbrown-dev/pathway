# Algebra I — Pathway mathematics

Course id: `algebra-1`, languageId `math` · Reviewed 2026-10-28 · Reference: [OpenStax Elementary Algebra 2e](https://openstax.org/details/books/elementary-algebra-2e)

## Purpose and prerequisites

Pre-algebra precedes Algebra I; Algebra I precedes Geometry, Algebra II and then Precalculus. This path does not assume prior knowledge of symbols beyond arithmetic, fraction operations, signed numbers, rates, and proportions. Those topics are reviewed here only when they unlock new understanding. The independent Precalculus course currently contains some introductory overlap, preserved for learners who enter there directly.

## Instruction and assessment rules

- Define new terms at first encounter, not after using them.
- Teach meanings, worked reasoning, and use cases before presenting an exercise. Guided lesson practice is separate from graded module-end checkpoints.
- Every letter has a meaning, every real-world quantity has a unit where relevant, and every result is checked against its original model.
- Negative solutions are **valid answers**. Learners may type a minus sign, use the ± control next to the numeric input, or insert a signed calculator result.
- All eleven modules have checkpoint questions and lesson-specific review links. Correct answers never appear in public lesson JSON.
- Interactive graphics for waves and phasors live in the Electrical Engineering AC sequence, with a related display in the Precalculus unit-circle lesson. They are not prerequisites for introductory Algebra I.

## Modules

1. **Algebra language:** From words to algebraic expressions; Terms and equivalent expressions; Distributive, associative, and commutative properties.
2. **Equations:** Solve equations with several operations; Variables on both sides and special cases; Rearrange formulas for the quantity you need.
3. **Inequalities:** Solve inequalities including negative coefficients; Compound inequalities and interval meaning; Build and interpret constraints.
4. **Linear graphs:** Slope as rate of change; Graph lines with slope and intercept; Parallel and perpendicular lines.
5. **Systems:** Systems and intersection points; Solve systems by substitution; Solve systems by elimination.
6. **Exponents and growth:** Exponent rules with positive and negative powers; Scientific notation and powers of ten; Exponential growth and decay.
7. **Polynomials:** Polynomial vocabulary and degree; Add and subtract polynomials; Multiply polynomials and special products.
8. **Factoring:** Factor out a greatest common factor; Factor quadratic trinomials; Difference of squares and zero-product rule.
9. **Quadratic functions:** Graph quadratics, vertex, and intercepts; Completing the square; Solve quadratic equations by formula and discriminant.
10. **Functions:** Functions, inputs, outputs, domain, and range; Transform a graph using shifts and reflections; Piecewise rules, absolute value, and boundaries.
11. **Applications and capstone:** Decide which model fits a situation; Fit lines to data and inspect assumptions; Capstone: compare two real pricing plans.

The final capstone compares two pricing plans, solves their break-even point, and checks the cheaper option in different quantity ranges. A later Algebra II course should expand complex numbers, logarithms, inverses, radicals, rational functions, and deeper polynomial theory.
